# Research Proposal

## Title
E-commerce Customer Purchase Behavior and Satisfaction Analysis

## 1. Background
Online retailers often monitor customer satisfaction and repeat-purchase intention. A relationship between these constructs should be evaluated using clearly defined measures and appropriate analysis rather than assumed. This project is an educational exercise using synthetic data.

## 2. Problem statement
The association between satisfaction and repeat-purchase intention is frequently discussed in e-commerce, but its strength and direction depend on context, measurement, and study design.

## 3. Aim
Examine the association between customer satisfaction and stated repeat-purchase intention in a hypothetical e-commerce survey.

## 4. Objectives
1. Describe satisfaction and repeat-purchase-intention scores.
2. Estimate the association between satisfaction and repeat-purchase intention.
3. Compare repeat-purchase-intention scores between lower- and higher-satisfaction groups.
4. Report statistical significance and effect size.
5. Identify limitations and improvements for a real study.

## 5. Research question
Does customer satisfaction significantly influence repeat-purchase intention in e-commerce?

For this cross-sectional practice dataset, “influence” is interpreted as **association**, not causal effect.

## 6. Hypotheses
- **H0:** Mean repeat-purchase-intention score is equal in lower- and higher-satisfaction groups.
- **H1:** The means differ.
- Significance level: alpha = 0.05, two-sided.

## 7. Literature review plan
Search Google Scholar, Scopus, or a university library with:
- “customer satisfaction” AND “repurchase intention” AND e-commerce
- “online shopping satisfaction” AND “behavioral intention”
- “e-commerce customer experience” AND loyalty

Record author, year, context, sample, constructs, method, findings, and limitations. Verify all sources and cite consistently (APA 7 is suitable). This ZIP does not fabricate references.

## 8. Methodology
- **Design:** Quantitative, cross-sectional survey (hypothetical).
- **Unit of analysis:** Individual adult online shopper.
- **Population:** Adults (18+) who purchased online in the previous six months.
- **Sampling:** For a real study, use a documented sampling frame and justified probability or non-probability strategy. The dataset here is synthetic.
- **Instrument:** Structured questionnaire with 5-point Likert items.
- **Variables:** Satisfaction (1–5), repeat-purchase intention (1–5), delivery rating (1–5), purchase frequency, age band.
- **Analysis:** Descriptive statistics, Pearson correlation, Welch t-test, Cohen's d.
- **Software:** Python, Pandas, NumPy, SciPy.

## 9. Ethics
Participation should be voluntary and informed. Explain purpose, duration, risks, data use, retention, and contact details. Avoid names, phone numbers, addresses, account IDs, and payment information. Participants may skip questions and withdraw before anonymized aggregation. Obtain institutional approval where required. The included data has no real participants.

## 10. Expected contribution
A reproducible workflow and template for a future real survey. Synthetic data cannot establish real-world customer behavior.

## 12. Limitations
- Synthetic data; no external validity.
- Cross-sectional self-report measures.
- Likert scores treated approximately numeric for demonstration.
- Convenience sampling may introduce selection bias.
- Correlation and group comparison do not establish causality.
- Grouping ordinal scores loses information and requires justification.
